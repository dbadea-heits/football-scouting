FOUR CORNERS PROFILE

TECHNICAL                                                     Rating: Adequate
Livaković's penalty-stop technique is elite — three saves against Japan in
WC 2022 round of 16, further heroics against Brazil. Multiple analyst
sources confirm soft hands that absorb and control the ball, excellent
footwork enabling quick lateral repositioning, and a balanced default
stance. The save% at Fenerbahçe (68.1% combined, 68.9% in Super Lig
2023-24) is adequate but not strong; it compares unfavourably to the
72-74% benchmark expected from top-quartile European GKs. At Dinamo
Zagreb (2025-26 HNL), FootyStats records 78.95% — a 10pp swing that
reflects both a weaker opponent pool and a more controlled environment.
Distribution has improved (once a known weakness) and now reaches 71%
pass accuracy at EL level; below the 75% constraint. First touch and
controlled catches are technical strengths.

TACTICAL                                                       Rating: Strong
The sweeper-keeper profile is the most defensible finding in this report —
corroborated by UEFA, coaching sources, and national team context. Livaković
positions high outside the six-yard box as his default starting position,
reads through-balls before they are played, and has been explicitly
described by multiple analysts as Croatia's chief offside-trap organiser.
Top speed of 24.5 km/h (UEFA EL tracking, 2024-25) is high for a
goalkeeper and extends the effective sweeping radius. His heatmap at
Fenerbahçe showed a wide distribution zone, consistent with a modern
GK-Libero role rather than a line-keeper. Decision-making in one-on-one
situations is described as composed and technically correct — narrowing
angles and timing correctly. The principal tactical concern is game-
management in lower-defensive-block moments — less documented.

PHYSICAL                                                      Rating: Adequate
Top speed 24.5 km/h (UEFA EL tracking) is excellent for position — most GKs
peak at 21-23 km/h; this places him in the top decile for positional
athlete output. Height of 186cm is adequate for aerial balls in behind,
though not exceptional for high-cross scenarios. The injury record for
2024-25 is the concern: three spells (muscle Dec 16–Jan 12, thigh Jan
14–23, general Feb 28–Mar 1) resulted in approximately 37 days missed
across the season — Medium risk by Transfermarkt thresholds. Two
muscular injuries in the same season is a pattern flag, not a single
incident. No career-threatening injury (ACL, surgery) on record.
