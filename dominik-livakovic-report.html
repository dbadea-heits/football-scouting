Rationale›

Livaković is a genuine archetypal sweeper-keeper whose tactical profile maps precisely onto a high-line/offside-trap system — he is Croatia's confirmed offside-trap organiser, a role he holds by merit across multiple competitive cycles. A constructive system gap exists: a structured high-line acquiring club may extract save performance materially above his 68% Fenerbahçe floor. Three concurrent flags prevent Shortlist: (1) the 68% save% when exposed behind the line is the worst-case failure mode for this specific system; (2) the December 2025 Girona incident confirms personal career priorities will override club obligations when stakes align — a reliability risk in a system requiring unconditional commitment from the last line of defence; (3) the Barcelona 2027/28 deal compresses any acquisition window to a single loan year. Primary risk is a Girona-style commitment failure during a decisive domestic run.

The clinching evidence is Croatia: Livaković's WC 2022 performances — 3 penalty saves vs Japan, heroics vs Brazil — emerged in exactly the high-line/organised-system context this Philosophy demands. He is not a generic sweeper-keeper; he is one calibrated to function best in a defensively coherent, line-setting environment.

Four Corners ◐ ● ◐ ○
Technical Adequate›

Penalty-stop technique is elite — three saves against Japan (WC 2022 round of 16) and further heroics against Brazil represent the highest-leverage technical performance a GK can deliver. Soft hands are consistently reported across coaching analyses: absorbs shot power, produces controlled rebounds, avoids second-chance scenarios. Footwork described as excellent, enabling fast lateral repositioning from a balanced default stance. Distribution has improved from an early-career weakness; now competently executes both short recycling passes and longer line-bypassing deliveries at 71% accuracy in EL competition.

The limitation is routine shot-stopping at club level: 68.1% save% (Fenerbahçe all comps 2024-25) and 68.9% (Super Lig 2023-24) sit at approximately the 50th percentile among European GKs, not the top-quartile level expected from a starting keeper in a high-stakes system. The high-line context generates direct shots when the trap breaks — this is exactly the scenario where Adequate, not Strong, is the honest rating.

Tactical Strong›

Sweeper-keeper profile is the most evidence-rich finding in this report. Multiple independent sources confirm: default starting position well outside the six-yard box; proactive sweeping of through-balls before they are played; confirmed as Croatia's chief offside-trap organiser across multiple competitive cycles. UEFA EL tracking records a top speed of 24.5 km/h, extending his effective sweeping radius significantly beyond most GKs. Described by multiple analysts as using "line-breaking distribution to bypass high-pressing modern attacking systems" — a tactical archetype precisely aligned with this Philosophy.

Communication and organisation are Strong: multiple coaching and media sources document him talking to defenders continuously, giving instructions, coordinating line movement. Míchel's public criticism at Girona, while behaviorally negative, confirms he is direct and confident in expressing his views — a quality that translates into defensive leadership on the pitch. Decision-making in one-on-one situations is described as technically correct (narrows angle, correct timing). The gap not well documented: game management in long spells of controlled possession rather than active sweeping.

Physical Adequate›

Top speed 24.5 km/h (UEFA EL tracking, 2024-25) is high for a goalkeeper — the typical GK ceiling is 21-23 km/h among European professionals. This places him in approximately the top 10-15% of GK athletic output and is the physical asset most directly enabling the sweeper-keeper role. Height 186cm provides adequate aerial coverage for balls played in behind; not exceptional for high cross situations but sufficient for the sweeping role where reactions and sprint pace matter more than aerial reach.

The injury record for 2024-25 is the Adequate-capping factor: three spells (muscle Dec 16–Jan 12 ~27 days, thigh Jan 14–23 ~9 days, general Feb 28–Mar 1 ~1 day) totalling approximately 37 days missed. Two muscular episodes in the same season is a pattern flag under Transfermarkt's Medium risk classification (5-15% matches missed or one significant muscular injury in last 24 months). No career-threatening injury history on record. The 2025-26 Dinamo Zagreb season (14 appearances without apparent interruption to date) is a positive sign, but one clean season does not clear the pattern flag.
