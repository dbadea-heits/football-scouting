Scouting Verdict

Deniz Undav — the pick. He scored 19 league goals, set up 7 more, and finished 3 goals clear of what his chances were actually worth. That kind of overperformance isn't a hot streak — it's a finisher who knows what he's doing in the box. He links up, he presses relentlessly, and at 28 he's entering the best years of his career. At roughly €20M he might be the best-value striker available in Europe right now. The only real question is whether he wants to make the move.

Orri Óskarsson — the bet. Nine goals in under 830 minutes is either a sign of something special or a very small sample from a 21-year-old coming off the bench. Probably both. The efficiency numbers are genuinely absurd — 70% shot accuracy, one in three shots hitting the net — but you can't fully trust nine games worth of data. He's already in La Liga, the Barcelona links haven't gone away, and the upside is huge. If you can stomach the uncertainty, no one in this group has a higher ceiling at the price.

Nicolò Tresoldi — the project. Twenty-one years old, nineteen goals, and he never stops working. He's physical, intelligent in his movement, and the pressing numbers put him near the top of the group. The legitimate hesitation is the Belgian Pro League — it's a step below La Liga and he hasn't faced defenders of this calibre yet. If the fee stays around €12M and there's patience in the plan, it's a bet worth taking.

Luis Suárez — the proven one. He scored 28 league goals. Not in a top-5 league, but the volume and consistency are hard to dismiss — 0.93 per 90, top scorer in Portugal, and his xG barely flatters him, which means the chances were real. At 28, the prime window is now. The step-up question is genuine, but Gyökeres already answered it from the same league. Worth a serious conversation.

Georges Mikautadze — the safe option. Already in La Liga, already knows what it takes to perform in it. Thirteen goals and six assists show he contributes across the board, not just as a poacher. He won't transform the attack, but he's reliable, available, and there's no adaptation period. If the club needs certainty over upside, he's the sensible call.

Ollie Watkins — the expensive ready-now. Sixteen Premier League goals, overperforming his xG, strong aerially and relentless in behind. Villa are reportedly willing to let him go for around €34M, which for a proven PL striker at 30 is arguably fair value. He's not a long-term solution given his age, but if Barcelona need someone who can perform from day one without any adaptation risk, Watkins is the realistic answer at the top of the budget.

Ayoze Pérez — useful, but not the answer. At 32 and playing roughly 45 minutes a game in a rotation role, Ayoze still shows he can contribute — five goals and four assists in La Liga is not nothing, and he knows the league inside out. But Barcelona need a starter and a presence, not an impact substitute. Fine as depth, not as the fix.

Nicolas Jackson — not at that price. The rate is respectable — 0.72 per 90 at Bayern in a limited role — but he's the only striker in this group consistently finishing below his xG, and that has followed him throughout his career. Factor in Chelsea's €65M obligation and the fact that Bayern seem happy enough letting him walk, and the value simply isn't there compared to the other options here.

Viktor Gyökeres — the benchmark, not a target. Arsenal paid €63.5M and he delivered 14 Premier League goals in a system that shared the load. He's useful as proof that Primeira Liga production translates to the top level, and not much else — he was never available and the price was always going to be beyond the conversation.

Charles De Ketelaere — a different question. Three league goals is not what Barcelona are shopping for, but 8.75 xA and his ability to unlock defences with his passing is a genuinely elite skillset. If the club wants to evolve the attack rather than just replace Ferran, CDK makes the conversation interesting. If they need goals, he doesn't.

Mikkel Bro Hansen — file for later. He's 17, he has two senior league goals, and 13 goals in 16 under-17 caps for Denmark is a serious number. He's not remotely ready for this conversation yet. Check back in two years.
